---
title: Stop saying “make a login page”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: Email, password, a button — that is only the skin. Name the stage first: a centered card, a split, immersive, a role gate, or steps.
related: login-taxonomy
---

“Make a login page” describes a form. What people actually hit is a gate that does not match the product.

A quiet tool is drawn as a full-bleed poster, so arrivals cannot find the card. A studio that needs to speak brand is only a white card in empty space. Travel or event atmosphere is boxed into a SaaS card, so the punch is gone. Personal and enterprise fields differ, but they are jammed into one long form. Email, verify, and password land on one screen, so one view does three jobs. They all look like login. They are not the same stage.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and how arrival stands.

- **Centered card**: the card floats in whitespace.
- **Split**: brand on the left, form on the right.
- **Immersive**: the form sits on the ground, not in a second column.
- **Role gate**: pick an identity, then enter its form.
- **Steps**: one job per screen, not one long form.

The pairs people mix up are Split and Immersive, Role gate and Steps. One is two panes; the other sits on the wash. One picks a role first; the other is one job per screen.

---
title: Stop saying “make a fancy hero”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: A big picture and two buttons is only the skin. Name what the first glance must answer: what it solves, who you are, why now, what is for sale, what happened, what you will learn, what it can do, or who is here.
related: hero-taxonomy
---

“Make a fancy hero” describes a big picture, a slogan, two buttons. What people actually hit is a first-glance job that does not match the page.

A product page is empty talk, so you cannot tell what it solves. A portfolio dumps a CV, so you cannot tell who they are or how good the work is. An event asks you to register before it says why join now. A shop rotates five posters, so the one product and the reason to buy disappear. A news home is packed with nav and subscribe, so you cannot tell what happened. A course lists a syllabus, so you cannot tell what you will learn to make. A tool tells a company story, and you cannot try it. A community sells the platform, and you cannot see who is here. They all look like heroes. They are not the same machine.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and what the un-scrolled glance must confirm.

- **Product**: what it can solve. Value on the left, product on the right, trust below.
- **Portfolio**: who you are, and how good the work is. Magazine banner, avatar, a piece of work.
- **Event**: why join now. Theme, when and where, one primary, seats.
- **Commerce**: what is for sale, and whether it is worth it. One product, not five posters.
- **Media**: what happened. Headline, a short summary, time and source.
- **Education**: what you will learn to make. An outcome and work slices, not a syllabus.
- **Tool**: what it can do for you. One line, try it here.
- **Community**: who is here. People and topics, not a pitch.

The pairs people mix up: commerce is not five posters; a course is not a syllabus. A shop features one product. A course promises the work.

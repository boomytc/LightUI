---
title: Stop saying “make a chart”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: Numbers on a drawing are only the skin. Name what this data is for seeing first: change, size, share, relation, flow, or ability.
related: chart-taxonomy
---

“Make a chart” describes numbers, therefore a drawing. What people actually hit is an intent that does not match the mark.

Months are sliced into a pie, so the rise and fall disappear. City names stand up as columns and rotate 45° to fit. Ad spend and sales become two lines, though the points are not a time series. Shown, click, and pay sit as sibling columns, so the drop is hidden. Six scores become six columns, so rounded vs lopsided never shows. They all look like charts. They are not the same machine.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and time before a line / axis from 0 / five slices max.

- **Change**: a line; volume too, an area. Equally spaced time; break a gap. Do not join unordered categories.
- **Compare**: short names, columns; long names or rank, bars. Axis from 0.
- **Share**: at most five slices, a donut from 12 o'clock. Not a trend.
- **Relate**: two continuous numbers, a scatter; two dimensions × intensity, a heatmap.
- **Flow**: a funnel. Each step contains the next.
- **Ability**: a radar. 5–7 axes, all 0–100, from 0.

The pairs people mix up: a trend is not a pie; a long name is not a column.

---
title: Stop saying “make tabs”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: A row of labels is only the skin. Name the selection first: a short bar, a joined panel, three-state steps, a sliding pill, stacked paper, or the thumbnail itself.
related: tab-taxonomy
---

“Make tabs” describes titles in a row, a click, a swapped panel. What people actually hit is a selection that does not match the job.

The underline on sibling sections fills the cell, so it scans like buttons. Members keep a bottom edge, so a seam sits between the tab and the panel. Checkout is drawn as sibling views, so you cannot see which step is done. Today / this month becomes another report, and the layout jumps. Project files bevel without stacking, so the cut is only decoration. Click a thumbnail, and the hero is a different picture. They all look like tabs. They are not the same machine.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and what selection changes.

- **Linear slider**: the bar follows the words, not the cell.
- **Card**: the current tab drops its bottom edge and joins the panel.
- **Chevron steps**: arrows bite; done / current / todo.
- **Segmented**: one track; a white pill slides to the click.
- **Folder**: stacked, beveled paper; the current tab sits on top.
- **Image preview**: the thumbnail is the tab; the hero is the same picture.

The three pairs people mix up: linear is not segmented; card is not folder; chevron is not sibling tabs.

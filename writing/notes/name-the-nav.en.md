---
title: Stop saying “make a navbar”
date: 2026-08-15
summary: A row of links is only the skin. Name where it lives, how it opens, and whether scroll pins, highlights, or shrinks it.
related: nav-taxonomy
---

“Make a navbar” describes a few destinations in a row. What people actually hit is a model that does not match the job.

A floating card uses `fixed` and covers the page. A breadcrumb is treated as the menu, so a deep page from search has no way out. A mega panel is squeezed to a slit on a phone. A drawer opens and the page underneath still scrolls. A spy flickers after a click. White type sits on a light hero while the bar is still transparent. They all look like nav. They are not the same machine.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and placement / reveal / scroll.

- **Floating sticky**: an inset card.
- **Sidebar**: vertical primary nav.
- **Breadcrumb**: a path, not the menu.
- **Dropdown**: one column of child pages.
- **Mega menu**: classified columns.
- **Drawer**: slides from an edge.
- **Overlay**: replaces the page.
- **Scrollspy**: follows the section.
- **Shrink**: changes the bar itself.

The pairs people mix up: a drawer is not a full-screen overlay; a dropdown is not a mega menu; shrink is not a floating sticky.

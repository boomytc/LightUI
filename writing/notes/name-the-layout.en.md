---
title: Stop saying “make a page”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: Blocks are only the skin. Name the skeleton first: single column, landing, masonry, full-screen, splitter, dashboard, or modular cards.
related: layout-taxonomy
---

“Make a page” describes blocks: a title, some cards, a main area. What people actually hit is a skeleton that does not match the content.

A long read is stretched full-bleed, so Chinese lines become unreadable. A conversion page is built as an admin dashboard, so the promise is crowded out by numbers. Pictures are cropped to one height, or become a carousel that swaps the whole view. A brand first screen is stuffed with three paragraphs, so the punch is gone. Two workspaces cannot be dragged, or they are built as a covering drawer. A page meant for scanning KPIs is written as marketing. One-idea-per-card collage is written as a single essay. They all look like pages. They are not the same machine.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and how this page is laid out.

- **Single**: one reading axis; figures and quotes share that column.
- **Landing**: hero promise → proof bands → CTA.
- **Masonry**: uneven; cards drop down the column.
- **Full-screen**: one shot fills the viewport; no body copy.
- **Splitter**: two work panes, a visible divide.
- **Dashboard**: a grid of KPIs, charts, and tables.
- **Modular**: one idea per card, on a grid.

The pairs people mix up: landing is not a dashboard; masonry is not a carousel; a splitter is not a drawer overlay; filling the viewport is not what the first glance answers.

---
title: Stop saying “pop a toast”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: A line of copy is only the skin. Name the interruption weight first: a glance, auto-dismiss, an undo, a log, or must-handle.
related: notify-taxonomy
---

“Pop a toast” describes a line of copy appearing on screen. What people actually hit is an interruption weight that does not match the job.

An unread count is built as a dialog you must dismiss. A successful save blocks the form until you click OK. Delete asks “are you sure?” instead of doing it and offering undo. A leaked-key risk is gone in two seconds. They all look like notices. Must they see it, must they act, can they miss it — not the same weight.

Ask three things first: the name, the scene, and how loudly it should interrupt.

- **Badge**: only a number; unload at 0, no empty dot.
- **Toast**: only a result; gone in about 2.4s, the form keeps moving.
- **Snackbar**: do it first; undo for about five seconds.
- **Marquee**: copy rotates in one strip; pause on hover.
- **Inbox**: write it to the bell; look it up later.
- **Alert**: pinned in the content until handled.
- **Banner**: under the nav, across pages, until they close it.

The three pairs people mix up: a notice is not a dialog; a marquee is not a carousel; an alert is not a toast. An empty state is not a notice, and neither is progress.

---
title: Stop saying “make a carousel”
date: 2026-08-24
summary: A set of frames that change is only the skin. Name the cut first: a slide, a fade, a coverflow, a stack, a page turn, an accordion, a 360 spin, or parallax.
related: carousel-taxonomy
---

“Make a carousel” describes a few pictures in a group that move on. What people actually hit is a cut that does not match the motion they mean to commit.

A brand hero is built as a hard sideways slide, so it flashes a knife-cut. A portfolio window is flattened onto a track, so the sides have no volume. Recommendation cards become a banner, so the top one cannot be peeled. A lookbook is translated, so there is no spine. A product 360 is four side photos sliding left and right. They all look like carousels. They do not commit the same motion.

Ask what the motion commits.

- **Classic slide**: whole-page translate; dots stay bound to the page.
- **Fade**: opacity only, no translation.
- **3D coverflow**: center large; sides turn with volume.
- **Card stack**: peel the top card, not a horizontal track.
- **Page flip**: turn on the spine; both pages stay in view.
- **Accordion gallery**: one expands, others shrink.
- **360° spin**: rotate the product; drag is an angle.
- **Parallax**: layers at different speeds, not a single-layer slide.

The pairs people mix up: advancing frames is not a notice marquee; a carousel is not masonry; list pagination is not a marquee.
